Basis & assumptions

  1. One total, two halves. The invoice adds up everything that came in and everything that went out; net profit is the difference, and the partner share is 25% of that. There are no sub-totals to reconcile — every line on the page is already inside net profit exactly once.
  2. Ticket sales = completed bookings only (tickets delivered), by completion date in July, Singapore time. Refunded/cancelled bookings excluded.
  3. Price per ticket is derived, not a list price — a blended average. Actual charges vary by rider: standard fares alongside custom friends-&-family, beta-tester and bug-tester rates.
  4. FX — blended actual. 0.32026 SGD/RM = total SGD paid to top up the KTMB card ÷ RM received, across all July top-ups. The 1 Jul top-up deferred from June (settled 3 Jul, RM 5,000 / SGD 1,600.51) is included here.
  5. Processing fee — blended actual. 5.0246% = July Airwallex payment fees ÷ gross deposits, applied to each route's revenue. Refund fees (SGD 35.50) excluded — they relate to refunded bookings, not sold tickets.
  6. Infrastructure is a flat SGD 500.00/month shared cost, not attributable to a single route, so it is deducted once at the total level.
  7. Deposits ≠ revenue. SGD 84,485.00 was collected in deposits; the excess over revenue is unspent rider wallet balance (a liability) and is excluded.
  8. Terminated bookings (348). Ticket was bought then cancelled: rider is refunded 50% (you keep 50%) and KTMB refunds 50% of the fare. Net = ½ revenue kept − ½ fare = +SGD 1,140.99. The 50% KTMB refund is assumed — the actual amount is not stored in our system (only in the KTMB eWallet); confirm there for an exact figure.
  9. Wasted processing fee. SGD 3,182.00 was withdrawn by riders in July; the inbound Airwallex fee on that money (5.0246%) earned no ticket revenue, so SGD 159.88 is booked as cost.
  10. Boost (priority) fees. A flat SGD 10.00 queue-jump charge, billed as its own ledger entry and not folded into the booking price — which is why it appears as its own "money in" line rather than inside ticket sales. 135 were charged (SGD 1,350.00); 49 more were granted free and earn nothing. It arrives as a wallet deposit like any other payment, so the same 5.0246% processing fee applies — that fee is the separate "money out" line of SGD 67.83, leaving SGD 1,282.17 net.
  11. Withdrawal fees. SGD 77.76 kept across 47 of 75 payouts (the fee shipped 7 July 2026, so earlier payouts carry none). Collected on the way out, so it bears no inbound gateway fee — distinct from the wasted-fee line above, which is a cost on the same money.
  12. Giveaways and manual corrections. SGD 28.00 of promotional credits handed to riders as goodwill — a real giveaway, so it is money out. Net SGD 5.00 paid out by BunnyBooker from hand-made corrections between the operating account and rider wallets.
  13. Surcharges and discounts are already inside ticket sales. A late-booking surcharge raises the fare the rider pays and a discount lowers it, so neither is separate income or a separate cost — they are shown indented under ticket sales as working, never as their own line in the total. The base fare is SGD 10.00 on every booking, so it reconciles to the cent: 4,777 tickets × 10.00 = 47,770.00, plus 585.00 of surcharges, less 590.00 of discounts, gives exactly the SGD 47,765.00 shown. Figures are derived from what each rider actually paid, so all 4,777 tickets are covered — not from the stored price breakdown, which only began mid-July 2026 and would leave part of the month unmeasured.
  14. Duplicate bookings (37, SGD 371.00 refunded) net to zero and are excluded from profit. Every one has no recorded KTMB cost — no ticket was ever bought — so refunding the rider costs nothing beyond the processing fee already accounted for. Listed for completeness.
  15. Profit-share split. The agreed 50% marketing share (SGD 16,011.73) is divided equally between 2 partners at 25% each. This invoice bills one 25% share; the net profit and all workings are identical for both.
  16. Already collected outside BunnyBooker — an advance, not a cost. July had 0 boosts granted at no charge on the partner account and 159 tickets bought on it. That money was collected from riders directly and never entered a BunnyBooker account, so it changes none of the figures above: the net profit is SGD 32,023.46 either way and the 25% share is still SGD 8,005.87. What it changes is the transfer. SGD 477.00 (0 × 10.00 plus 159 × 3.00) is already in the partners' hands, SGD 238.50 of it this partner's, so that much is treated as paid and only SGD 7,767.37 is transferred.
